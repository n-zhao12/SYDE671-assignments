Part One · A camera field exercise

Perspective is a point of view.

These experiments explore how camera position and focal length change the appearance of a scene. The goal is to notice the difference—not just change the zoom setting.

01 · Portrait: close versus far

The same subject is photographed once from close range and again from farther away with zoom, keeping the face at approximately the same size in the frame.

Close-up portrait

Stepped-back, zoomed portrait

Observation: The close viewpoint exaggerates perspective differences across the face. Moving farther away and zooming in preserves similar framing while producing more natural facial proportions.

02 · Architectural perspective compression

The same street scene is photographed from a distant, zoomed-in viewpoint and a closer, wider-angle viewpoint, with approximately similar framing.

Farther away, zoomed in

Closer, without zoom

Observation: The distant, zoomed view compresses apparent depth, making near and far parts of the scene appear more similar in scale. The closer viewpoint emphasizes perspective and the relative size difference between foreground and background.

03 · Dolly zoom

The animated sequence combines stills captured while moving the camera backward and zooming in, keeping the subject roughly the same size in the frame.

My dolly-zoom sequence

Takeaway: Perspective is determined by camera position; focal length changes framing. Moving the camera while adjusting zoom therefore changes the spatial relationships visible in the image, even when the subject stays a similar size.
