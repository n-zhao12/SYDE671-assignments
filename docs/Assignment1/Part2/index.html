Part Two · Images of the Russian Empire

Colorizing the Prokudin-Gorskii photo collection

Sergei Prokudin-Gorskii recorded scenes on glass plates through red, green, and blue filters. This project separates the stacked exposures, aligns them, and combines them into color images.

See the resultsView source code

The problem

Each grayscale scan contains three exposures stacked vertically in blue, green, red order. Small shifts between exposures produce colored fringes unless the channels are aligned before combination.

01 · Split

Extract channels

Divide each plate into three equal-height sections: blue is the reference, and green and red are the moving channels.

02 · Align

Search translations

Estimate an (dx, dy) shift for green and red relative to blue. Compare valid overlapping interior pixels, not wrapped pixels.

03 · Reconstruct

Combine and improve

Apply shifts, crop to the shared valid region, combine channels, then rescale contrast for display.

Alignment methods

The implementation includes exhaustive single-scale search and a coarse-to-fine image pyramid.

Single-scale baseline

For each candidate translation, compare the reference and shifted channel. Raw NCC mean-centers the image regions and scores their normalized dot product; the best-scoring translation is selected.

We chose NCC because the separate filtered exposures can have different brightness levels. Mean-centering and normalization make the score focus more on shared image structure than on absolute intensity.

Single-scale NCC outputs available for two low-resolution images

Coarse-to-fine pyramid

Repeatedly downsample by two, find a coarse translation, scale it back up, and refine locally at each finer resolution. This makes a wider effective search practical for large scans.

The reported vector is the shift applied to the moving channel.

Texture-weighted NCC

An extension weights pixels by Sobel gradient magnitude in the blue reference, giving detailed regions more influence than smooth areas. Basic NCC results are included separately.

Bells & whistles

Starting with raw NCC, we explored ways to make channel alignment less sensitive to distracting borders and to emphasize useful image structure. The selected improvement was a 5% edge exclusion; we then tested two alternative NCC feature strategies. All final images are also cropped to their shared valid area and automatically contrast-stretched to the 0–255 range. Each comparison uses the same three images—00458u, 01007a, and 01657u. 1. Add 5% edge exclusion — final selected method

What it does: At every candidate translation, NCC is computed only on the valid overlap after ignoring a 5% margin along its edges. The image channels themselves are not pre-cropped by this margin; it changes which pixels contribute to the alignment score.

Why we tried it: Plate frames and channel borders can have unusual or mismatched intensities. If included, these pixels may dominate the score even though they are not useful scene features. Ignoring a small edge band lets NCC focus more on the interior image content.

Observed result: The 5% setting produced better alignments than scoring all the way to the overlap edges in our comparisons, so we selected raw NCC with 5% edge exclusion for the main results.

Building on that improvement, we next tested alternative NCC features: For these experiments the edge exclusion remains at 5%, while the data representation used by NCC changes.

2. Gradient-magnitude NCC — negative impact in our tests

What it does: Sobel filters produce a gradient magnitude image for each channel, and NCC compares those gradient images instead of the original pixel intensities.

Why we tried it: Edges and gradients describe structural boundaries and may be less sensitive to brightness differences between the color-filter exposures. We expected matching structure to help where raw intensity patterns differ.

Observed result: It aligned less reliably than our selected raw-NCC method in the images reviewed. Gradient magnitude can amplify noise and discards some intensity information, so the structural signal was not consistently more useful than raw intensities for these plates.

3. Texture-weighted NCC — little visible difference

What it does: A weight map is computed from Sobel gradient magnitude in the blue reference. NCC still compares original intensities, but detailed pixels receive higher weights than smoother areas.

Why we tried it: Distinctive textures and edges can provide strong alignment cues. We expected emphasizing those regions to reduce the influence of broad, smooth areas and improve the shift estimate.

Observed result: The outputs looked very similar to selected raw NCC, with no consistent visible improvement. The unweighted score already aligned the main structures in these examples, so giving textured regions extra influence did not materially change the result.

Takeaways from the experiments

Selected method

The pyramid narrows full-resolution search to local refinements. Overlap-aware scoring and edge exclusion reduce false matches from rolled pixels and plate borders. Texture weighting emphasizes detailed regions.

Alternative metrics

Gradient-magnitude NCC and texture-weighted NCC were evaluated against the selected method. Gradient NCC was less reliable on the examples reviewed, while texture weighting produced similar-looking results. These comparisons helped confirm raw NCC with 5% edge exclusion as the method used for the main result set.
