Main takeaway

Registration quality depends on both geometry and evidence.

In Part 1, each homography comes from manually paired landmarks; in Part 2, local appearance proposes matches and RANSAC keeps the geometrically consistent subset. In both cases, the transform maps one image into a shared projective frame, while interpolation and blending determine how the resampled views look at their seams.

Three overlapping sequences

One scene, multiple views

The three image sets provide the inputs for the manual and automatic panoramas. A projective panorama assumes the camera rotates about an approximately fixed center of projection; camera translation and scene depth differences introduce parallax, which a single homography cannot remove.

See the source sequences alongside their manually stitched results in Part 1 A.1, and the automatic results in Part 2 B.4.
